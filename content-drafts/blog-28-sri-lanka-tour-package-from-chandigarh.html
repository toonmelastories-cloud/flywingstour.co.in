Getting There from Chandigarh

There is no direct Chandigarh-Colombo flight, but the routing improved meaningfully in 2026. IndiGo now operates six direct weekly flights between Delhi and Colombo, and Air India runs daily flights on the same route, so the trip from Chandigarh is a short domestic hop to Delhi followed by one direct international leg, roughly four hours in the air. That is a noticeably easier routing than it was even a year ago, when most Sri Lanka trips out of North India involved a longer connection through a southern Indian hub.

The Spine Route: One Journey, Not a Checklist

Sri Lanka is compact enough that the country's best-loved circuit is genuinely one continuous route rather than a set of disconnected stops. Here is how it runs, and why each leg earns its place.

Leg One: Colombo, 1 Day

Most visitors treat Colombo as an arrival point rather than a destination, and that is broadly the right call for a first trip. A half day covers the essentials, Gangaramaya Temple, the Pettah Market, Galle Face Green at sunset, before heading inland the next morning.

Leg Two: Sigiriya and Dambulla, 1 to 2 Days

An inland detour before the hill country, built around Sigiriya, the ancient rock fortress rising dramatically out of the jungle, and the Dambulla cave temples nearby. This leg is optional if your trip is under a week, but it is the single most photographed site in the country and worth the extra day if you have it.

Leg Three: Kandy, 1 to 2 Days

The cultural heart of the hill country, home to the Temple of the Sacred Tooth Relic, one of Buddhism's most significant pilgrimage sites, and the Royal Botanical Gardens at Peradeniya. Kandy is also where the trip's signature journey begins.

Leg Four: The Kandy to Ella Train, Half a Day

This is the leg that anchors the entire route, and it is worth planning the rest of the itinerary around rather than treating as an afterthought. The train climbs through tea plantation country, misty hills, and waterfalls, crossing the Nine Arch Bridge, arguably Sri Lanka's most photographed piece of infrastructure, on the way. Book this leg well in advance, observation-car and reserved seats sell out weeks ahead in peak season, and an unreserved standing journey is a very different experience from a seated one on a five-hour ride.

Leg Five: Ella, 1 to 2 Days

A small hill town that has become a backpacker and honeymooner favourite in equal measure. Little Adam's Peak is a manageable sunrise hike with big views, and the surrounding tea estates make for an easy, unhurried couple of days after the train journey.

Leg Six: The South Coast, 2 to 3 Days

Down from the hills to beaches, typically Mirissa or Bentota depending on the vibe you want, surf-and-whale-watching energy at Mirissa, calmer resort energy at Bentota, plus Galle Fort, a UNESCO-listed colonial old town with ramparts, a lighthouse, and a genuinely different architectural feel from anywhere else on the route.

A Sample 8-Day Itinerary

This is the shape most of our Chandigarh clients end up booking, the full spine route paced to avoid feeling rushed at any single leg.

  • Day 1: Arrive Colombo, half-day city tour, Gangaramaya Temple and Galle Face Green sunset
  • Day 2: Drive to Sigiriya, afternoon climb of the rock fortress, evening at Dambulla cave temples
  • Day 3: Drive to Kandy, Temple of the Sacred Tooth Relic, evening cultural dance show
  • Day 4: Morning at Peradeniya Botanical Gardens, board the afternoon train to Ella through tea country
  • Day 5: Full day in Ella, Little Adam's Peak sunrise hike, Nine Arch Bridge, tea estate visit
  • Day 6: Drive to the south coast, check into Mirissa or Bentota, evening at leisure
  • Day 7: Beach day, optional whale watching from Mirissa (best November to April) or a Galle Fort half-day trip
  • Day 8: Morning at Galle Fort if not covered the day before, drive to Colombo for departure

This pacing keeps each leg to a comfortable one or two nights and builds the train journey in as a genuine highlight rather than a rushed transfer, which is the single most common mistake in self-planned Sri Lanka itineraries.

Extending the Trip: Yala National Park

For anyone with ten days or more, a Yala National Park safari is worth adding between the south coast and Colombo. Yala has one of the highest leopard densities of any national park in the world, alongside elephants, sloth bears, and a huge range of birdlife. Most visitors add one or two nights near the park and go out on early-morning and late-afternoon safari drives, when animal activity is highest and the heat is more manageable. This add-on suits wildlife enthusiasts specifically rather than every traveller, and it does add real driving time to the route, worth weighing against simply extending the beach leg instead if wildlife is not a priority.

Sri Lanka for Family and Group Trips

The spine route described above works comfortably for multi-generational family groups with a couple of adjustments. Older travellers do well skipping the Sigiriya rock climb in favour of the Dambulla cave temples, which involve far less physical exertion for a comparable cultural payoff, and the train journey itself is genuinely one of the more relaxing legs of any Sri Lanka itinerary, seated, scenic, and requiring no walking at all. For larger friend groups and younger travellers, the country's affordability relative to Thailand or Bali means the budget stretches further across a bigger group without compromising on hotel quality, and the range of activities, hiking, surfing, wildlife, temples, all within a compact geography, tends to keep a mixed-interest group satisfied without anyone feeling like the itinerary was built around someone else's preferences.

What to Eat

Sri Lankan food shares real common ground with South Indian cuisine, which makes it one of the more comfortable international food cultures for Indian travellers to adjust to immediately.

  • Rice and curry, the everyday meal structure, typically several vegetable and protein curries served alongside rice, similar in spirit to a South Indian thali but with a distinct coconut-and-spice profile
  • Hoppers (appa), bowl-shaped rice-flour pancakes, often served with an egg cracked into the centre for breakfast
  • Kottu roti, chopped flatbread stir-fried with vegetables, egg, and meat or paneer, cooked with a distinctive rhythmic chopping sound that street stalls are known for
  • Fresh seafood, particularly along the south coast, where the day's catch typically reaches the table within hours
  • Ceylon tea, grown in the hill country you will pass through on the train, worth a proper tasting stop at one of the estates around Ella or Nuwara Eliya

Vegetarian travellers generally do very well here, given the structural overlap with South Indian vegetarian cooking, and most restaurants on the tourist circuit are used to accommodating Indian dietary preferences without difficulty.

Who This Route Is Not For

Worth saying honestly. If a single-resort, minimal-movement beach holiday is what you actually want, a Maldives or a Goa trip suits that better than Sri Lanka's multi-stop circuit, which does involve genuine road time between legs. If mobility is a serious concern, the Sigiriya rock climb and the Little Adam's Peak hike both involve real physical effort, though both have easier alternative viewpoints nearby for anyone who wants the scenery without the climb. For most travellers wanting a short-haul trip that combines culture, hill country, a genuinely memorable train journey, and beach time in one affordable week, this remains one of the strongest value propositions available from North India right now.

Packing and Practical Notes

  • Sri Lankan Rupee is the local currency; cards are widely accepted in Colombo and tourist hotels but carry cash for smaller towns and the train journey.
  • Dress modestly for temple visits, shoulders and knees covered, and be prepared to remove footwear before entering, including at the Temple of the Sacred Tooth Relic in Kandy.
  • Book the Kandy-Ella train and any peak-season hotels well ahead, particularly around Sri Lankan and Indian holiday overlaps, when both domestic and Indian tourist demand spike together.
  • Mobile data is easy to arrange on arrival, with tourist SIM cards available at Bandaranaike International Airport, useful given how much of the route runs through hill country with patchy signal.

Booking Timing

Two things are worth acting on sooner rather than later this year specifically. First, the free-ETA change is genuinely new enough that awareness is still building, meaning flight and hotel pricing has not yet fully adjusted to the demand increase it is likely to bring, booking now captures pricing from before that adjustment happens. Second, the Kandy-Ella train's limited reserved seating means peak-season dates, particularly around Indian festival long weekends, need booking weeks rather than days in advance. Neither is urgent in the way a limited-time discount would be, but both reward planning a few weeks ahead of instinct.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Sri Lanka really visa-free for Indians now?

Yes, since May 25, 2026, the ETA required for Indian tourists is completely free, though you still need to apply for it online before travelling. It is free of cost, not free of the application step.

Is there a direct flight from Chandigarh to Sri Lanka?

Not from Chandigarh directly, but Delhi now has direct flights to Colombo on both IndiGo and Air India, so the trip is a short domestic hop to Delhi followed by one direct international leg of around four hours.

Do I need to book the Kandy to Ella train in advance?

Yes, strongly recommended. Reserved and observation-car seats on this route sell out weeks ahead in peak season, and travelling without a reservation usually means standing for a significant part of the roughly five-hour journey.

How many days does a Sri Lanka trip need?

Seven to eight days covers the full classic route, Colombo, Kandy, the hill-country train, Ella, and the south coast, comfortably. Five to six days works if you skip the Sigiriya detour.

Is Sri Lanka good for a honeymoon?

Yes, particularly the Ella and south coast legs, which offer a slower pace and a good mix of hill-country romance and beach time within a single short trip.

What is driving the recent surge in Jaffna specifically?

Jaffna's deep Tamil heritage, improved direct connectivity from southern India, and its position in the dry zone during the north Indian summer months have all contributed to a sharp rise in interest, though it suits a different kind of traveller, heritage and culture focused, than the classic hill-country-and-beach route this guide is built around.

Is Sri Lanka safe for solo and women travellers?

Yes, Sri Lanka is generally considered one of the safer destinations in the region for tourists, including solo and women travellers, with a well-established tourist infrastructure along the main circuit. As with any international trip, standard precautions apply, particularly around late-evening travel in unfamiliar areas.

Can this trip be combined with the Maldives?

Yes, and it is a genuinely popular combination given how close the two destinations sit to each other. Colombo has reasonable onward connectivity to Male, making a Sri Lanka-plus-Maldives combined itinerary a realistic option for travellers with ten days or more who want both a cultural circuit and pure beach time in one trip.
