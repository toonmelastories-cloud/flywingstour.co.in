Baku's Two Skylines

Old City (Icherisheher), the UNESCO Side

A walled medieval core at the heart of modern Baku, and a UNESCO World Heritage Site in its own right. The Maiden Tower, a cylindrical stone structure with panoramic city views from the top, and the Shirvanshahs' Palace, a well-preserved royal residence, anchor a neighbourhood of narrow cobblestone lanes that feels centuries removed from the rest of the city, despite sitting a short walk from it.

Flame Towers and Modern Baku, the Futuristic Side

Three curved glass towers shaped like flames, illuminated at night with a shifting light display, dominate Baku's skyline from almost every angle in the city. The Heydar Aliyev Center, an architecturally striking cultural building with no straight lines in its design, reinforces the same theme, a country deliberately projecting a modern, forward-looking identity alongside its genuine historical core.

Most first-time visitors expect one or the other to be the "real" Baku. In practice, the city's appeal is precisely that both exist within walking distance of each other, and a well-paced itinerary gives real time to both rather than treating one as a quick photo stop on the way to the other.

The E-Visa Process

  • Applied for entirely online, through the official ASAN e-visa portal, no embassy visit required for Indian nationals
  • No visa-on-arrival, the e-visa must be approved before you travel
  • Standard processing typically takes a few working days, with an expedited option available for a higher fee if you are booking closer to your travel date
  • Stays beyond roughly two weeks require registration with the local migration authority, worth confirming if your trip runs longer than a standard holiday

Fee figures for both standard and expedited processing vary across sources we checked while researching this guide, so rather than quote a number that may already be outdated, we would recommend confirming the current fee directly on the official portal or with our visa desk before applying.

Beyond the City: Gobustan and the Mud Volcanoes

A genuinely strange and memorable day trip from Baku. Gobustan National Park, a UNESCO-listed site, holds rock carvings estimated at thousands of years old, a direct window into the region's ancient human history. Nearby, Azerbaijan's mud volcanoes, small cone-shaped formations that bubble and occasionally spit cool, thick grey mud, are a genuinely unusual sight found in very few places worldwide. The Ateshgah Fire Temple and Yanar Dag, a hillside where natural gas seeps constantly burn, round out a full-day excursion built around Azerbaijan's long association with fire and flame, a theme that also explains the Flame Towers' design back in the city.

What to Eat

Azerbaijani cuisine sits at a genuine crossroads of Turkish, Persian, and Caucasus influences, built around grilled meats, rice dishes, and fresh herbs.

  • Plov, Azerbaijan's signature rice dish, saffron-infused and typically layered with meat, dried fruit, or herbs, with regional variations worth comparing across different restaurants
  • Kebabs, grilled meat skewers, a staple across the country and widely available from street vendors to proper restaurants
  • Dolma, grape leaves or vegetables stuffed with spiced rice and meat, a genuine local specialty rather than a borrowed dish
  • Azerbaijani tea culture, served in distinctive pear-shaped glasses, often with jam stirred in rather than sugar, a hospitality tradition worth experiencing properly rather than rushing
  • Baklava and local sweets, widely available and reflecting the same Turkish and Persian culinary crossover found throughout the cuisine

Vegetarian travellers should plan ahead, meat features heavily in traditional Azerbaijani cooking, though Baku's more cosmopolitan restaurants increasingly offer vegetarian options.

Packing and Practical Notes

  • Pack for wind regardless of season, Baku is genuinely one of the windier major cities in the region, and a light windbreaker is worth including even in summer.
  • Azerbaijani Manat is the local currency; cards are widely accepted in central Baku but carry cash for the Old City's smaller shops and the Gobustan day trip.
  • Dress modestly for religious sites, though Baku itself is a modern, cosmopolitan city with no strict general dress code for everyday sightseeing.
  • English is reasonably well spoken in tourist areas of Baku, though Russian remains widely understood as a second language, useful if travelling with someone who speaks it.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do Indians need a visa for Azerbaijan?

Yes, a standard e-visa applied for online in advance through the official ASAN portal. Visa-on-arrival is not available for Indian passport holders.

Is there a direct flight from Chandigarh to Baku?

Not from Chandigarh directly, but Delhi has direct flights to Baku, primarily on Azerbaijan Airlines, at roughly four and a half to five hours, so the trip is a short domestic hop to Delhi followed by one direct international leg.

How many days does a Baku trip need?

Four days covers the Old City, the Flame Towers and modern Baku, and a Gobustan day trip comfortably. Three days works for a shorter version focused purely on the city itself.

What makes Baku's mud volcanoes worth visiting?

They are a genuinely rare natural phenomenon, found in very few locations worldwide, and the Gobustan area combines them with ancient UNESCO-listed rock art in a single accessible day trip from the city.

Is Azerbaijan good for a honeymoon?

Yes, the combination of a romantic historic old town and a striking modern skyline, all within a short, direct flight from Delhi, has made it an increasingly popular short honeymoon destination for Indian couples.

What is the best time to visit Baku?

April to June and September to October are generally the most comfortable windows. Baku is known for strong winds year-round, worth packing for regardless of season.

Is Baku safe for solo and women travellers?

Yes, Baku is generally considered a safe, cosmopolitan city for tourism, with a well-established tourist infrastructure and a genuinely welcoming attitude toward visitors in the main tourist areas.

Can this trip be combined with Georgia?

Yes, given both sit in the wider Caucasus region, though the connectivity between Baku and Tbilisi involves either a flight or a long overland journey, worth planning carefully if combining both in one trip with limited days.
