Getting There from Chandigarh

Gulmarg

Fly Chandigarh to Srinagar, roughly 414 kilometres by air, then a road transfer of about 50 kilometres to Gulmarg itself, an hour to ninety minutes depending on road conditions. Winter flight schedules into Srinagar can shift with weather, morning arrivals occasionally reshuffled during specific wind patterns, worth building a little schedule flexibility into your travel days.

Auli

Auli has no airport of its own. The route runs through Dehradun's Jolly Grant Airport, followed by a road journey to Auli, or a cable car ropeway from nearby Joshimath for the final stretch. Chandigarh to Auli by road runs roughly 495 kilometres, a genuinely long drive if you choose that route directly rather than flying to Dehradun first.

Our honest read on connectivity: Gulmarg is the easier trip logistically from Chandigarh specifically, a single flight plus a short transfer, while Auli requires either a longer combined flight-and-road journey or a full road trip.

Who Should Pick Gulmarg

  • Intermediate to advanced skiers who want genuine terrain variety and the option to progress across a full week rather than exhausting the slopes in a day or two
  • Anyone specifically chasing powder conditions, Gulmarg's natural snowfall in a good season is among the best in Asia for recreational skiing
  • Groups wanting a broader Kashmir trip alongside the skiing, Srinagar's Dal Lake and Gulmarg's own gondola rides work well as non-ski activities on rest days, and our full Kashmir tour package guide covers the wider circuit if skiing is only part of your plan
  • Travellers comfortable with a slightly longer, more structured booking process given Gulmarg's higher demand and more established tourist infrastructure

Who Should Pick Auli

  • First-time skiers and families with children, given the gentler, more compact terrain and a generally calmer overall atmosphere
  • Travellers who want skiing as one part of a broader scenic trip rather than the entire focus, Auli's Himalayan views and the Joshimath cable car ride are worth the visit even for non-skiers in the group
  • Anyone prioritising a quieter, less crowded winter destination over the more developed, busier Gulmarg experience during peak weeks
  • Groups already planning a Char Dham-adjacent Uttarakhand circuit who want to add a winter sports stop without a separate trip to Kashmir

  • Day 4: Return to Joshimath and Dehradun for departure

Beyond Skiing: What Else Each Destination Offers

Worth knowing if your group is not entirely made up of skiers, since neither destination requires everyone to be on the slopes to enjoy the trip.

Gulmarg's Non-Ski Options

The Gulmarg Gondola, one of the highest cable cars in the world, is a genuine attraction on its own, offering panoramic Himalayan views for anyone happy to ride up and simply take in the scenery rather than ski down. Snow-shoeing, sledging, and simply walking through Gulmarg's meadows under fresh snow are all realistic non-ski activities for a rest day or for travelling companions who are not skiing at all.

Auli's Non-Ski Options

The Auli-Joshimath ropeway, one of the longest cable car rides in Asia, is worth the trip alone for the views, and the Auli artificial lake, built originally to support snowmaking, offers a striking frozen or snow-rimmed viewpoint depending on conditions. Auli's slower overall pace makes it a genuinely relaxed base for non-skiers compared to Gulmarg's busier peak-season atmosphere.

Gear and Packing

  • Rental equipment is available at both destinations, skis, boots, and poles, so bringing your own gear is optional unless you have specific fit or performance preferences.
  • Layer aggressively regardless of destination, thermal base layers, a mid-layer fleece, and a waterproof outer shell handle the temperature swing between exertion on the slopes and standing around at the lift queue.
  • Good sunglasses or ski goggles are not optional, snow glare at altitude causes genuine eye strain and, in bad cases, snow blindness without proper eye protection.
  • Waterproof gloves and boots matter more than most first-time winter travellers expect, wet hands and feet end a ski day faster than cold alone.

Safety Notes for First-Time Skiers

Both destinations offer certified instructors and designated beginner slopes, and starting there rather than attempting an intermediate run on day one is worth the ego cost. Altitude is a secondary factor at both, Gulmarg's higher elevation runs in particular, so pacing yourself on the first day rather than pushing straight to full-day skiing helps avoid both altitude fatigue and the physical strain of a sport most first-timers are not conditioned for. Weather can close lifts or restrict runs on short notice at either destination, worth building a buffer day into a short trip rather than assuming every planned ski day will go ahead as scheduled.

Gulmarg and Auli for Family and Group Trips

Auli's gentler terrain and generally calmer atmosphere make it the more straightforward pick for families with young children or mixed-experience groups where not everyone skis at an advanced level. Gulmarg works well for families too, particularly with its dedicated beginner areas, but the broader terrain and busier peak-season crowds suit a more independent, experienced group slightly better. For friend groups specifically, Gulmarg's heli-skiing options and bigger terrain give more experienced skiers room to push themselves while beginners in the same group stick to the lower slopes, a genuine advantage of scale that Auli's more compact terrain cannot quite match.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which is better for beginners, Gulmarg or Auli?

Auli generally suits first-time skiers and families better, given its gentler, more compact terrain. Gulmarg is also workable for beginners on its designated slopes, but its broader terrain and higher-altitude runs are more geared toward intermediate and advanced skiers.

Which has better snow, Gulmarg or Auli?

Gulmarg, clearly. Kashmir's weather patterns deliver heavier, more reliable natural snowfall than Uttarakhand's, and Gulmarg's powder skiing in a good January is considered among the best in Asia.

Is Gulmarg or Auli easier to reach from Chandigarh?

Gulmarg, by a meaningful margin. A single flight to Srinagar followed by a short road transfer beats Auli's combination of a flight to Dehradun plus a longer road journey or the alternative of a full 495-kilometre drive.

When does the 2026-27 ski season start?

Both destinations typically open as early as late November, with mid-December through late February as the prime window for reliable snow at both resorts.

Can complete beginners learn to ski at either destination?

Yes, both Gulmarg and Auli have ski schools offering lessons for complete beginners, with designated learner slopes separate from the more advanced terrain.

Is one destination significantly cheaper than the other?

Gulmarg's more developed infrastructure tends to push costs somewhat higher for a comparable trip, though Auli's longer, more complex transfer from Chandigarh can narrow that gap once total trip cost is factored in.

Is heli-skiing available, and at which destination?

Heli-skiing is a genuine, growing option at Gulmarg specifically, aimed at advanced skiers wanting access to terrain beyond the standard lift-served slopes. Auli does not currently offer a comparable heli-skiing programme.

What happens if there is not enough snow when we arrive?

Both destinations can see variable snow conditions, particularly in the early and late parts of the season, which is part of why the mid-December to late-February window is considered the safer bet for reliable snow cover, and why building a flexible itinerary with non-ski activities as a backup is worth doing regardless of which destination you choose.
