Somewhere in the last few years, the question changed from "where should we go on holiday" to "where can I actually get work done while feeling like I am on holiday." Over sixty percent of Indian travellers now actively look for exactly this, and the honest answer for anyone reading this from Chandigarh is that some of India's best workation hubs are closer than most people realise. At Flywings Tour & Packages Pvt Ltd, we get asked about this constantly, usually by someone who has just realised their team is fully remote for a week and wondering whether that week has to happen at a kitchen table, or whether it could happen somewhere with a view instead. This guide compares the hill stations that actually work for it, not just the ones that look good in a listicle, ranked by the questions that actually determine whether the trip succeeds rather than the ones that make for a prettier photo.

Quick context: A workation only works if the WiFi does. This guide ranks Chandigarh-reachable hill destinations by connectivity reliability and distance first, scenery second, which is the opposite order most travel content uses and the order that actually matters once you are three hours into a client call.

What Actually Matters for a Workation, in Order

  1. Reliable internet, not just available internet. A property advertising WiFi and a property with fibre-backed WiFi that survives a video call are two very different things.
  2. Distance from Chandigarh, since a workation with a six-hour drive each way eats into the working days you actually have available.
  3. A proper desk and chair, not a bed and a view. This sounds obvious and is the single most common disappointment first-time workation bookers report.
  4. Backup power, since hill towns see more frequent outages than plains cities, and a property without a generator or inverter can lose you an afternoon without warning.
  5. Everything else, the view, the cafes, the hiking trails, matters enormously for the experience, but only after the first four are actually sorted.

Kasauli: The Test Run

If you have never done a workation before, Kasauli is the honest place to start. It is close enough that a failed experiment costs you a weekend, not a week, and close enough that if something urgent comes up at the office, Chandigarh is under two hours away rather than a full day's travel. The trade-off is that Kasauli feels less like a destination workation and more like a change of scenery, which for a first attempt is exactly the point.

McLeod Ganj: The Established Choice

Dharamshala and McLeod Ganj have the most developed remote-work culture of any option on this list, which shows up in practical ways: cafes that expect laptop workers rather than tolerate them, more reliable connectivity because more of the local economy depends on it, and enough of a working community that meeting other remote workers happens naturally rather than requiring effort. It is also far enough from Chandigarh that this genuinely needs to be a proper trip, not a long weekend squeezed around a normal work week.

Bir Billing: Work Hard, Fly Harder

Bir Billing has built its reputation on paragliding, and that reputation is well earned, but it is increasingly showing up in workation searches for a specific reason: the same qualities that make it a great paragliding base, a real town with real infrastructure rather than a scenic viewpoint with a few hotels, also make it workable for remote work. The honest caveat is that connectivity here is genuinely improving rather than already excellent, so this is the destination on this list where checking a specific property's actual internet setup before booking matters most.

Tirthan Valley: Beautiful, But Be Honest About Why You Are Going

Tirthan Valley is stunning, and it is also the entry on this list where we are most likely to talk a client out of treating it as a primary workation base. Connectivity here is patchier than the other options, which makes it wonderful for a genuine digital detox with light, asynchronous work, and frustrating for anyone whose week involves scheduled video calls. Know which trip you are actually planning before you book.

Manali: The Long-Stay Option

For workations measured in weeks rather than days, Manali has the most developed infrastructure of anywhere on this list, including a genuine co-living scene that did not exist a few years ago. It is also the furthest from Chandigarh, which means the travel time only makes sense once amortised across a longer stay. A three-day Manali workation rarely feels worth the journey. A three-week one often does.

Beyond the Big Five: Underrated Options Worth Watching

The hubs above are the reliable, well-tested choices, but a few less obvious names are worth knowing about, particularly if you want somewhere quieter with fewer other remote workers competing for the same cafe tables:

  • Bundi in Rajasthan is further afield but increasingly mentioned in workation circles for its heritage architecture and a slower pace, better suited to a longer trip that starts or ends in Chandigarh via Delhi.
  • Coorg and Kodaikanal, further south, come up often in national workation content but are a genuine flight rather than a drive from Chandigarh, worth considering only if the trip itself, not just the working days, is the point.
  • Chikhaldara in Maharashtra is flagged as an emerging option nationally, though realistically too far for a Chandigarh-based short workation and better suited to travellers already in that region.

For most Chandigarh-based remote workers, the Himachal hub comparison above covers the genuinely practical options. These further-flung picks are worth knowing about mainly for a longer, dedicated trip rather than a regular workation rotation.

Workation vs Bleisure: Two Different Trips

It is worth distinguishing a workation from the bleisure trend covered in our corporate travel guide, since they solve different problems. Bleisure typically means extending an existing business trip by a few personal days at the same destination. A workation is a dedicated trip built entirely around working remotely from somewhere other than home or office, for its own sake, with no business trip attached to it. Both are growing quickly among Indian professionals, but they need slightly different planning, and conflating them tends to produce a trip that does neither job particularly well.

What to Actually Pack for a Workation

  • A portable WiFi hotspot or a second SIM on a different network, as backup for exactly the property WiFi outage you did not plan for.
  • A laptop stand and an external keyboard, since working from a bed for a week is a fast route to a sore neck and slower typing.
  • A power bank rated for laptop charging, not just phone charging, for the hill towns where outages are more common than in the plains.
  • Layered clothing, since most of these destinations run noticeably cooler than Chandigarh, especially in the evenings.
  • Noise-cancelling headphones, for the call you need to take from a cafe or a shared co-living space.

Booking the Right Kind of Stay

Not every hotel or homestay in these towns is workation-ready, even when the listing says it has WiFi. What separates a genuinely workable stay from a disappointing one:

  • A dedicated desk in the room, confirmed before booking rather than assumed from photos.
  • Recent reviews mentioning WiFi speed specifically, not just its presence, since a property can technically have WiFi that cannot handle a video call.
  • Co-living or co-working focused properties in Manali and McLeod Ganj specifically, which are increasingly purpose-built for exactly this traveller rather than repurposed tourist accommodation.
  • A cafe or two within walking distance as a genuine backup plan, not a nice-to-have.

Weekday vs Weekend: Planning Around the Actual Work

The travellers who get the most out of a workation treat the travel days deliberately, arriving a day before the work week starts rather than trying to work through arrival-day travel fatigue, and building in at least one genuine day off mid-stay rather than treating every single day as a normal office day in a nicer location. Solo Workation vs Team Workation

The planning conversation changes noticeably depending on whether you are going alone or bringing a small team, and it is worth being clear about which one you are actually organising before booking anything.

  • Solo workations prioritise café culture and a light sense of community over dedicated meeting space, since most solo remote workers are on calls rather than hosting them, and value being somewhere with other people around rather than fully isolated.
  • Small team workations, increasingly requested by startups and small departments wanting a change of scenery together, need a property with an actual meeting room or a large common area with reliable WiFi that can handle several simultaneous video calls, not just one.
  • Team workations also need group logistics, shared transport from Chandigarh, rooms clustered together rather than scattered, and a single point of coordination, similar in spirit to the group travel principles covered in our group travel desk, just for a work trip rather than a wedding or pilgrimage.

A property that works beautifully for one person quietly working through a laptop on a café balcony can fall apart the moment five colleagues try to run a client call from the same WiFi connection at the same time. This is worth checking with the property directly rather than assuming capacity that was never actually tested.

Talking to Your Manager About a Workation

For employees rather than founders or freelancers, the workation conversation often needs to happen with a manager first, and it goes better with specifics rather than a vague request. Naming the exact destination, the connectivity plan including a backup option, and confirming which days involve meetings versus focused work tends to land better than a general ask to "work remotely for a bit." Several of our corporate clients, covered in more depth in our corporate travel guide, have started formalising exactly this kind of request into their travel policy, which is worth checking if your company already has a managed travel programme in place.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which is the best workation destination near Chandigarh?

It depends on trip length. Kasauli suits a short trip or a first attempt, McLeod Ganj suits a week-long stay with an established remote-work culture, and Manali suits multi-week stays where the longer travel time is worth amortising across more working days.

How reliable is WiFi in Himachal hill towns?

It varies significantly by town and even by specific property. McLeod Ganj and Manali have the most developed, reliable connectivity on this list. Bir Billing is improving. Tirthan Valley is genuinely patchier and better suited to lighter, asynchronous work.

How far in advance should I book a workation stay?

Two to four weeks ahead is usually enough for these destinations outside peak season, though properties with dedicated co-working setups in Manali and McLeod Ganj can book up faster since demand for exactly that setup keeps growing.

Can Flywings help plan a workation trip, not just a holiday?

Yes, we increasingly get exactly this request, and the planning conversation is slightly different from a normal leisure trip. Tell us how many days are genuinely work days versus leisure days, and we will match the destination and property accordingly.

Is a workation worth it for a trip shorter than a week?

For destinations under two hours away like Kasauli, yes. For anything requiring a five-plus hour drive, the travel time starts to outweigh the benefit on a trip shorter than about five days.

What is the difference between a workation and a bleisure trip?

A bleisure trip extends an existing business trip with a few personal days at the same destination. A workation is a standalone trip built entirely around remote work somewhere other than home, with no business trip attached. They solve different problems and are usually planned differently.

Can a small team do a workation together, not just one person?

Yes, and this is a growing request from startups and small departments. It needs a different kind of property, one with genuine meeting space and WiFi capacity for several simultaneous calls, plus group transport and room coordination similar to any group trip.
