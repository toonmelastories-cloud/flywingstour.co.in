Quick context: VFS Global runs the visa application centres for most countries' Indian missions, including Chandigarh, and slots are released by each destination country's own system, first come, first served. Shortage isn't a Chandigarh-specific glitch, it reflects genuine demand, but a few practical habits make a real difference, and a few shortcuts are worth avoiding entirely.

Why Slots Disappear So Fast

VFS doesn't control how many appointment slots exist, each country's consulate or embassy decides the allocation, and VFS simply operates the booking system on their behalf. Slots are often released in batches rather than all at once, which is why refreshing the page at a specific time of day sometimes works better than checking randomly throughout the day. Peak travel seasons, summer and the winter holiday window in particular, see the sharpest demand spikes, and Schengen countries tend to run tighter than others because of shared quota limits across the zone.

What to Do Before You Even Try Booking

  • Complete the destination country's own online application first. Most VFS booking systems need a reference number from the country's official visa portal before a slot can even be selected, applicants who skip this step waste time discovering it mid-booking
  • Prepare your documents in full beforehand, so you can move quickly the moment a slot appears rather than scrambling to scan a missing paper
  • Book every family member in the same session where possible, linked bookings keep a family's appointments on the same day, which gets harder to arrange once slots are booked separately
  • Check neighbouring VFS centres, depending on the destination country's jurisdiction rules, a centre in a nearby city sometimes has more availability than Chandigarh on a given day

The Scam to Watch For

Search for "VFS appointment Chandigarh" and you will find no shortage of people and pages offering to get you a slot for a fee. Some of this is legitimate assistance, document preparation and appointment booking on your behalf, which is a normal part of what a visa desk does. Some of it is not. Paid slot-finder services that use automated bots to grab appointments violate VFS's terms, and a booking obtained that way can be cancelled without warning, sometimes after you've already taken leave and arranged travel around it. A few warning signs worth knowing:

  • Anyone asking for your VFS or visa portal login credentials should raise an immediate flag, a legitimate assistant should not need to log in as you
  • Guaranteed slots for an unusually high fee, no legitimate service can guarantee a government-controlled allocation, genuine help is about preparation and speed, not guarantees
  • Only applicants can collect the passport after processing, if someone claims they can collect it for you without proper authorisation, that's a red flag

Processing Timelines, Once You Have the Appointment

Getting the appointment is only the first hurdle, processing time after submission varies significantly by destination. Based on what our desk sees regularly, UAE applications typically move in three to four working days, Singapore around a week, Schengen countries and the UK usually need three to six weeks during peak season, and US visas depend heavily on interview slot availability, which can itself involve a wait. We always quote the realistic current timeline before you commit to travel dates, since published processing times and actual experience can diverge during busy periods.

What If You Still Can't Find a Slot?

  • Check at different times of day, early morning and late evening sometimes catch batch releases that get missed during the day
  • Widen your date range, if your travel dates allow flexibility, a wider acceptable window increases the odds of catching an available slot
  • Ask about premium or priority services, several VFS centres offer a paid premium lounge or priority appointment option directly through the official system, worth checking if your timeline is tight, since this is different from a third-party slot-seller and goes through VFS's own legitimate channel
  • Consider whether your trip dates can shift, sometimes the honest answer is building in more lead time rather than fighting an oversubscribed system

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does VFS keep showing "no appointment slots available"?

Slot numbers are set by each destination country's consulate, not VFS itself, and demand regularly outpaces supply during peak season. Slots are often released in batches at specific times rather than continuously throughout the day.

Is it safe to pay someone to book a VFS appointment for me?

Legitimate document preparation and appointment assistance is normal and safe. Paying for a guaranteed slot obtained through bots or unofficial means violates VFS's terms and the booking can be cancelled without warning. Never share your portal login credentials with anyone offering this.

Can I book my visa appointment before completing the online application?

No, most VFS booking systems require a reference number generated by the destination country's own official visa application first. Attempting to book before this step usually fails.

Does a travel agent get different or better VFS slots than individuals?

No, agents use the same official booking system as everyone else. What a good visa desk actually adds is a complete, ready-to-submit file and consistent monitoring for genuine releases, not special access.

What happens if my documents are incomplete when my appointment arrives?

Most centres will not accept an incomplete file, meaning the appointment is wasted and a new one has to be booked, often weeks later during busy periods. This is why document preparation before booking matters as much as the booking itself.

Can I change my VFS centre if Chandigarh has no slots?

Sometimes, depending on the destination country's jurisdiction rules for which centres can serve which applicants. Check the specific country's portal, since this varies and isn't available for every destination.
