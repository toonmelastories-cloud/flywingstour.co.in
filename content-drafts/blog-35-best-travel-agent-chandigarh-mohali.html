The Checklist

1. How Long Have They Actually Operated in the Tricity?

Longevity in this specific business matters more than in most, because the advantages that make an agency worth using, consolidator fare access, airline relationships, embassy documentation familiarity, genuinely take years to build. An agency that opened last year cannot have the same depth of relationships as one that has been booking Chandigarh-Delhi-Dubai routes since 2005. Ask directly, and be specific about whether they mean the agency or just the current owner's tenure, since agencies do change hands.

2. Do They Have a Physical Office You Can Walk Into?

Not because online-only agencies are automatically untrustworthy, but because a real office in Phase 7 Mohali, Sector 17 Chandigarh, or wherever they operate from signals a level of accountability that a purely digital storefront does not. If something goes wrong with your booking, being able to walk in and speak to someone in person is worth knowing you have as an option, even if you never need to use it.

3. Do They Handle Your Specific Trip Type Regularly?

A generalist agency and one that regularly handles group bookings, corporate travel, or visa-heavy international trips are not the same thing, even if both technically offer all three. Ask specifically, "how many group bookings of this size have you handled in the last year," or "how many Schengen visa applications have you processed," rather than accepting a general "yes, we do that" answer.

4. Can They Explain Their Pricing, Not Just State It?

A trustworthy agency will break a quote down by component, flights, hotels, transfers, visa fees, and explain why a particular routing or hotel category was chosen. An agency that only offers a single bundled number and gets defensive when asked to break it down is a fair reason to keep comparing.

5. Is There a Single Accountable Contact, or a Rotating Queue?

Ask who specifically will handle your booking from start to finish, and whether that is the same person you will reach if something needs fixing mid-trip. Agencies large enough to have multiple desks sometimes lose the single-point-of-contact advantage that is a big part of why people choose an agent over an OTA in the first place.

6. Do They Handle Visa Documentation In-House?

Some agencies outsource visa processing entirely to a third party, which adds a layer of communication delay and reduces accountability if something is filled out incorrectly. An agency with its own visa desk, rather than a pass-through arrangement, generally catches documentation issues earlier and follows up more reliably.

7. How Do Their Online Reviews Actually Read?

Star ratings alone tell you very little, a 4.5 built on eight reviews means something different from a 4.5 built on three hundred. Read a sample of the actual written reviews rather than just the number, specifically looking for mentions of group bookings, visa handling, or how the agency responded when something went wrong, since those are the exact scenarios this checklist cares about. A pattern of reviews praising responsiveness during a problem is worth more than a pattern of reviews only mentioning a smooth, uneventful booking, since the latter tells you nothing about how the agency handles the harder cases.

8. Do They Offer Genuinely Customised Itineraries, or Only Fixed Packages?

Some agencies operate almost entirely off a catalogue of pre-built packages with minimal flexibility. Others will build an itinerary around your specific dates, budget, and interests from scratch. Neither approach is automatically wrong, a well-priced fixed package can be excellent value for a standard trip, but know which one you are getting, and ask directly whether changes to a package's hotel category, duration, or included activities are genuinely possible or just a marketing claim.

Red Flags Worth Knowing

  • Reluctance to put anything in writing, a quote, a cancellation policy, an itinerary, that only exists verbally is a real risk, regardless of how confident the conversation sounds.
  • Pressure to book immediately without time to compare, a legitimate deal with genuine time pressure (a specific fare closing) is different from generic urgency tactics used regardless of actual availability.
  • Vague answers about who handles a problem after booking, if the agency cannot clearly explain what happens if your flight is cancelled, that gap will matter exactly when you need it least.
  • No verifiable physical address or years of operation, a legitimate, established agency will not hesitate to confirm both clearly.

Matching the Agency to the Occasion

Beyond the general checklist, certain trip occasions benefit from an agency that specifically specialises in them, worth asking about directly rather than assuming general competence covers it.

  • Honeymoons benefit from an agency that understands the difference between a generic beach package and a genuinely romantic itinerary pacing, private transfers, well-chosen hotel categories, activities that suit a couple rather than a family group.
  • Destination weddings need an agency comfortable coordinating dozens or hundreds of guest bookings, room blocks, and often multiple vendor relationships at the destination itself, a meaningfully different skill set from booking a single family's holiday.
  • Pilgrimage and yatra travel benefits from an agency with specific route knowledge, altitude and health considerations for high-altitude yatras in particular, and established relationships with local operators at the destination.
  • Corporate travel needs policy compliance tracking and reporting most leisure-focused agencies are not set up to handle well, worth confirming an agency has genuine B2B experience rather than treating a corporate booking like an oversized leisure trip.

Ask any agency you are considering directly how many bookings of your specific occasion type they have handled recently, rather than accepting a general "we do everything" answer at face value.

What a Good First Phone Call Actually Sounds Like

Beyond the checklist itself, the tone of an initial conversation tells you a lot. A good first call spends more time asking questions than pitching, who is travelling, what the occasion is, what has gone wrong on past trips if any, before recommending anything specific. It should end with you having a clear next step, a quote by a specific time, a document to send over for a visa assessment, not a vague "we'll be in touch." And it should not feel like the agent is reading from a script regardless of what you actually said, a genuine conversation adjusts to your specific answers rather than following the same pitch for every caller. If a first call feels rushed or generic, that is a reasonable preview of what the rest of the relationship will feel like.

A Note on Registered Business Details

Beyond the checklist above, a legitimate travel agency in India should be able to provide its registered business name and GST registration on request for any significant booking, particularly for corporate travel where an invoice needs to match company records. This is a small, practical detail rarely mentioned in generic "best of" lists but genuinely worth confirming before a large payment, since a legitimate registered business has nothing to hesitate about here and an unregistered or informal operation may.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a bigger, more well-known agency always better?

Not necessarily. Size sometimes trades off against the single-point-of-contact advantage that makes a travel agent worth using over an OTA in the first place. A mid-sized, established local agency often delivers more personal accountability than a large national chain's local branch.

How do I verify how long a travel agency has actually operated?

Ask directly, and cross-check against their registered business address and any online reviews dated further back than a year or two, which is a reasonable signal of genuine longevity rather than a recently rebranded operation.

Should I choose an agency based on price alone?

No. The cheapest quote is not useful if it comes from an agency that cannot handle a problem mid-trip, or that outsources visa processing with no accountability if something is filed incorrectly. Weigh price against the checklist items above, not in isolation.

What is the biggest mistake people make when choosing a travel agent?

Trusting a generic ranked list without checking whether that agency's specific specialty, leisure, corporate, group, visa-heavy, actually matches their own trip. The "best" agency for a solo trip and the "best" for a fifty-person wedding group are frequently not the same one.

Does Flywings only work with Chandigarh, Mohali and Panchkula clients?

Our office is based in Phase 7, Mohali, serving the full Tricity and wider Punjab region, but we regularly work with clients booking from further afield who found us through a specific package or guide rather than local proximity alone.

Should I trust a travel agency's own website testimonials?

Treat them as a starting point, not a verdict. Testimonials on an agency's own site are inherently selected to be favourable, which is not the same as being false, but cross-checking against independent review platforms and, ideally, someone you personally know who has used the agency gives a more complete picture.

Is it worth visiting a travel agency's office in person before booking a large trip?

For a significant booking, a group trip, a destination wedding, a corporate travel contract, yes. An in-person conversation reveals things a phone call or email exchange often does not, how organised the office genuinely is, whether the person you meet is the person who will actually handle your booking, and whether the answers to the checklist questions above hold up when asked face to face.

What should I do if an agency refuses to answer these checklist questions clearly?

Treat that refusal as useful information and continue comparing other options. A legitimate, established agency confident in its own service should have no difficulty answering every question on this list directly.
