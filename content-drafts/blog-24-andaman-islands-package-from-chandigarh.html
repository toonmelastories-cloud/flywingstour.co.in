The question we get asked most about Andaman is not "should we go," it is "how many days do we actually need." That is a fair question, because unlike a Dubai or Bangkok trip where four days is comfortable and six is generous, Andaman has a built-in time cost before the holiday even starts. There is no direct flight from Chandigarh, the islands themselves are separated by ferry crossings, and the weather half the year makes the whole trip a bad idea. This guide is built around that one decision, how many days to block on the calendar, because getting it wrong is the single most common reason people come back from Andaman saying it felt rushed.

Quick context: Indian citizens do not need any special permit to visit Port Blair, Havelock, or Neil Island, just a valid photo ID. The permit confusion you have probably read about online applies to foreign nationals and to restricted tribal areas, not to a standard Indian tourist's itinerary. More on this below, because it is worth clearing up properly.

Why Trip Length Is the Real Planning Question

Every other packaged destination we write about, Dubai, Bali, Singapore, is reachable in one flight leg or close to it, so the "how many days" question is mostly about budget and appetite. Andaman is different for two structural reasons.

  • There is no non-stop flight from Chandigarh to Port Blair. Every itinerary connects through a hub city, and the connection itself eats the better part of a travel day in each direction.
  • The islands are not one place. Port Blair, Havelock (officially Swaraj Dweep), and Neil (officially Shaheed Dweep) are separate islands linked by ferry, and each one earns its own day or two rather than being a stop on a single loop.

Add those two facts together and a 4-day Andaman trip and a 9-day Andaman trip are genuinely different holidays, not the same holiday compressed or stretched. Picking the wrong one is where the disappointment comes from, not the destination itself.

Getting There: What "From Chandigarh" Actually Means

There is no direct Chandigarh to Port Blair flight, and there is unlikely to be one anytime soon given the route economics. Every itinerary out of Chandigarh routes through one of three hub cities, and which one you connect through changes your travel day meaningfully.

Hub RouteTypical FrequencyWhat to Know
Via Chennai (MAA)Most frequent, roughly 5 to 6 Chennai-Port Blair flights dailyThe most reliable connection with the most schedule flexibility if your first leg runs late
Via Kolkata (CCU)Moderate, roughly 2 to 3 daily onward flightsOften the shortest total distance flown, useful if your Chandigarh-Kolkata leg has good timing
Via Delhi (DEL)Frequent Chandigarh-Delhi hop, then a connecting flight to Port Blair from DelhiEasiest first leg since Chandigarh-Delhi flights run constantly, but check the layover window carefully

Total journey time typically runs five to seven hours of flying plus a one to two hour layover, so realistically the entire first day and the entire last day of any Andaman trip are travel days, not island days. This is the single biggest planning mistake we see: people count arrival day and departure day as beach days when booking, then feel short-changed once the trip is underway. Build your itinerary length around the days you will actually have on the sand, not the total nights booked.

Permits: What Is True and What Is Outdated Internet Advice

This is worth its own section because so much of what circulates online about Andaman permits is either outdated or was never true for Indian citizens in the first place.

Traveller TypeWhat They Actually Need
Indian citizens visiting Port Blair, Havelock, Neil, Ross Island, North BayNo special permit. A valid government photo ID (Aadhaar or equivalent) is sufficient, the same as any domestic trip.
Foreign nationals visiting the standard tourist circuitA Restricted Area Permit (RAP), typically issued on arrival or online, valid for around 30 days
Anyone wanting to visit tribal reserve areas (home to the Jarawa, Sentinelese, Onge, or Great Andamanese communities)Not permitted for casual tourism at all. These areas are protected and regulated by the Andaman & Nicobar Administration, and responsible operators will not offer or encourage this.

If an itinerary, forum post, or travel group message is telling you that you as an Indian tourist need a special Andaman permit for Port Blair or Havelock, that information is wrong or badly outdated. Carry your ID, book your ferry tickets in advance during peak season, and that is the extent of the paperwork.

Pick Your Trip Length

These are the three shapes an Andaman trip realistically takes. None of them is the "correct" one, they suit different travellers and different amounts of leave.

4 Days: The Long Weekend Dash

This works if Andaman is a long-weekend add-on rather than a main annual trip. It only covers Port Blair and one island, usually Havelock, and it moves fast.

  • Day 1: Arrive Port Blair, afternoon at Corbyn's Cove or a relaxed evening, Cellular Jail light and sound show
  • Day 2: Morning ferry to Havelock, check in, Radhanagar Beach for sunset
  • Day 3: Full day on Havelock, snorkelling or a glass-bottom boat trip at Elephant Beach
  • Day 4: Morning ferry back to Port Blair, departure

The honest trade-off: you will see genuinely beautiful water and come home tired, because two of your four days are travel-adjacent. This length works for a couple squeezing in a trip around a long weekend, not for a first Andaman visit if you want it to feel unhurried.

6 Days: The Standard Trip

This is the itinerary most first-time Andaman visitors should book, and it is what most travel agents mean by default when they say "Andaman package."

  • Day 1: Arrive Port Blair, settle in, Cellular Jail visit
  • Day 2: Ferry to Havelock, afternoon at Radhanagar Beach
  • Day 3: Havelock water activities day, snorkelling, scuba trial dive, or Elephant Beach
  • Day 4: Ferry to Neil Island, Natural Bridge and Laxmanpur Beach at sunset
  • Day 5: Morning on Neil, afternoon ferry back to Port Blair, North Bay or Ross Island (Netaji Subhas Chandra Bose Dweep) if time allows
  • Day 6: Departure

This length gets you all three main islands without feeling like a checklist, and it leaves enough slack that a delayed ferry or a rained-out afternoon does not collapse the whole trip. If you are asking us for a default recommendation, this is it.

9 Days: The Deep Dive

Worth it for honeymooners, divers, or anyone who considers a beach holiday genuinely wasted if it is not unhurried. This length adds real diving time, a slower pace on each island, and room for weather buffer days, which matter more in Andaman than most destinations since ferries do get cancelled in rough seas.

  • Extra nights on Havelock for certified diving courses (multi-day certifications need the extra time)
  • A buffer day built in specifically to absorb a ferry cancellation without losing a paid night elsewhere
  • Time for Baratang Island's limestone caves and mangrove creek, a full-day excursion most 6-day itineraries skip entirely
  • A slower Neil Island stretch, which rewards unhurried mornings more than any other island in the group

What a Package Should Actually Include

Because Andaman involves inter-island movement in a way most domestic trips do not, the line items that matter are slightly different from a standard hill-station or beach package elsewhere in India.

  • Confirmed ferry tickets between islands, booked in advance for your specific dates, not "arranged on arrival." Peak season ferries sell out.
  • Hotel transfers on each island separately, since Port Blair, Havelock, and Neil each need their own local transport, this is not one continuous road trip.
  • Clarity on which activities are included versus optional add-ons, particularly scuba diving and the North Bay/Ross Island boat circuit, which are commonly quoted separately.
  • A realistic buffer day if you are travelling October to May at the shoulder edges, since even calm-season ferries occasionally get rescheduled for tide and weather reasons.

What Each Island Is Actually Known For

A common mistake we see in self-planned itineraries is treating Port Blair, Havelock, and Neil as interchangeable beach stops. They are not, and knowing what each one is genuinely good for helps you decide how many nights to give each one.

Port Blair: History, Not Beaches

Port Blair is where you land, and most visitors treat it purely as a transit point, which undersells it. The Cellular Jail, built by the British and used to imprison Indian freedom fighters, runs a light and sound show in the evening that is genuinely moving rather than a tourist formality. Corbyn's Cove offers a passable beach for an afternoon, but the real reason to give Port Blair a proper day rather than rushing through is the history, the Anthropological Museum, and the Samudrika Naval Marine Museum if you have an extra half day.

Havelock (Swaraj Dweep): The Water Activities Hub

Havelock is where most of the postcard Andaman photos come from, and it earns that reputation. Radhanagar Beach was rated among Asia's best beaches by international travel media for good reason, wide white sand, clean turquoise water, and a sunset that does not need filters. This is also the island for anyone wanting scuba diving, whether a one-time trial dive or a full PADI or SSI certification course, since most of the certified dive schools operate here rather than on Neil or in Port Blair. Elephant Beach, reachable by a short boat ride or a jungle trek, is the go-to for snorkelling and glass-bottom boat rides.

Neil (Shaheed Dweep): The Slow Island

Neil is smaller, quieter, and deliberately less developed than Havelock, which is exactly its appeal. The Natural Bridge, a limestone rock formation exposed at low tide, and Laxmanpur Beach's sunset are the two fixed points most itineraries build around, but the real value of Neil is the pace. It is the island to give an unhurried morning to rather than pack with activities, and honeymooners in particular tend to rate it as the highlight of the trip precisely because there is less to do, not more.

Practical Packing and Prep Notes

  • Carry a physical printout of your ID and hotel bookings, since mobile connectivity between islands can be patchy and you do not want to be searching for a PDF at a ferry counter.
  • Book inter-island ferries the moment your dates are fixed, especially December through February, since seats on the faster private ferry operators sell out well ahead of the sailing date in peak season.
  • Reef-safe sunscreen is not optional, several dive and snorkel operators now check for it before letting you in the water, since ordinary sunscreen damages the coral.
  • Cash still matters outside Port Blair, since ATMs and card acceptance get noticeably less reliable on Havelock and especially Neil Island.
  • Pack for both sun and sudden rain even in the dry season, since a passing shower on an otherwise clear day is common and will not derail plans if you are prepared for it.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do Indian tourists need a permit for Andaman?

No. Indian citizens need only a valid photo ID to visit Port Blair, Havelock, Neil Island, and the standard tourist circuit. No Inner Line Permit, Restricted Area Permit, or advance paperwork is required for the areas any standard itinerary covers.

Is there a direct flight from Chandigarh to Port Blair?

Not currently. Every route connects through Chennai, Kolkata, or Delhi, with Chennai offering the most frequent onward flights to Port Blair.

How many days should a first-time Andaman trip be?

Six days is the sweet spot for a first visit, enough time to properly cover Port Blair, Havelock, and Neil Island without feeling rushed, while keeping the trip length manageable against annual leave.

Is Andaman good for a honeymoon?

Yes, particularly November through February, when seas are calmest and the weather is at its most reliably pleasant. Havelock's Radhanagar Beach and Neil Island's quieter pace are the two stretches honeymooners consistently rate highest.

Can Andaman be done as a budget trip?

Yes, though budget flexibility matters most on flights and hotel category rather than on activities, since ferry tickets and entry fees are fixed regardless of hotel tier. Travelling in the January to March window also tends to offer better hotel availability and rates than the December peak.

What is the biggest mistake people make planning an Andaman trip?

Counting arrival and departure days as full beach days. Given the connecting-flight travel time from Chandigarh, both ends of the trip are largely travel days, and itineraries that do not account for this end up feeling rushed even when the nights-booked count looks generous.

Do I need to know how to swim to enjoy the water activities?

No. Snorkelling trips, glass-bottom boat rides, and trial scuba dives are all conducted with life jackets and an instructor or guide alongside you, so non-swimmers regularly take part safely. A full diving certification course does involve some in-water skills training, but the instructors build up to it gradually.

Is Baratang Island worth adding to the itinerary?

If you have the extra day, yes. Baratang's limestone caves and the mangrove creek boat ride are genuinely different from anything on Havelock or Neil, but it is a full-day excursion on its own and works best as an addition to a 9-day trip rather than squeezed into a 6-day one.
