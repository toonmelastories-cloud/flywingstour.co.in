Four Landscapes, One Short Trip

This is the detail that makes Oman genuinely stand out against other short-haul Gulf options. Most nearby international destinations from Punjab deliver one dominant experience, a city, a beach, a desert. Oman delivers all four within a country you can reasonably cross by road in a single well-planned week.

City: Muscat, 2 Days

The Sultan Qaboos Grand Mosque, best visited before 11 AM to beat both the heat and the crowds, is the essential stop, alongside the Mutrah Souq for spices, frankincense, and silver, and the Royal Opera House Muscat for a look at the city's more modern architectural face. Muscat itself is noticeably calmer and less built-up than Dubai, a genuine part of its appeal for travellers who want Gulf convenience without Gulf-city intensity.

Mountains: Nizwa and the Interior, 1 to 2 Days

A drive inland to Nizwa, once Oman's capital, centres on its imposing fort and a Friday-morning livestock and produce souq that feels genuinely local rather than staged for tourists. The surrounding Al Hajar mountains, including Jebel Shams, Oman's highest peak and known locally as the Grand Canyon of Arabia for its dramatic gorge views, reward an extra day if your itinerary allows it.

Desert: Wahiba Sands, 1 Night

An overnight stay at a Bedouin-style desert camp in the Wahiba Sands is the single experience most first-time visitors rate highest. Dune bashing by 4x4, a camel ride at sunset, and a night sky largely free of light pollution combine into an experience that genuinely does not exist anywhere within a similar flight time from Punjab.

Coast: Muscat's Beaches or a Salalah Extension

Muscat's own coastline offers pleasant beaches and dolphin-watching boat trips without needing to travel further, but travellers with extra days sometimes add Salalah in the south, a genuinely different Oman shaped by the annual khareef monsoon season, lush and green in a way that surprises people expecting only desert.

A Sample 6-Day Itinerary

  • Day 1: Arrive Muscat, evening at Mutrah Corniche and the souq
  • Day 2: Sultan Qaboos Grand Mosque in the morning, Royal Opera House and Old Muscat in the afternoon
  • Day 3: Drive to Nizwa, fort and souq visit, onward toward the Wahiba Sands by evening
  • Day 4: Overnight desert camp, dune bashing, camel ride, stargazing
  • Day 5: Morning desert sunrise, drive back to Muscat via Wadi Bani Khalid for a swim stop at the natural pools
  • Day 6: Muscat beach morning or last-minute souq shopping, departure

This pacing covers all four landscapes without feeling rushed, and it fits comfortably within a long weekend plus a couple of extra leave days, well suited to the kind of short-haul trip Amritsar's direct connection makes realistic in the first place.

What to Eat

Omani food is less internationally known than its Gulf neighbours' cuisines, but it is a genuine part of the experience once you know what to look for.

  • Shuwa, slow-cooked marinated lamb or goat, traditionally wrapped in banana leaves and cooked in an underground pit for close to a full day, usually reserved for special occasions but available at better restaurants
  • Majboos, a spiced rice dish with meat or fish, closely related to biryani in structure and a comfortable, familiar flavour profile for Indian travellers
  • Omani halwa, a dense, sticky dessert flavoured with cardamom, rosewater, and saffron, traditionally served alongside Omani coffee as a welcome gesture
  • Omani coffee (qahwa), lightly spiced with cardamom, served in small cups and refilled generously, a hospitality ritual worth experiencing rather than rushing through
  • Fresh seafood along the coast, including grilled kingfish and prawns, reflecting Oman's long coastline and fishing tradition

Indian restaurants are also widely available in Muscat given the large resident Indian community, useful for travellers wanting a familiar meal partway through the trip, though it would be a shame to skip Omani food entirely on a first visit.

Extending Into the Mountains: Jebel Shams and Jebel Akhdar

For travellers with an extra day or two, Oman's mountain interior rewards the detour. Jebel Shams, Oman's highest peak, overlooks a dramatic gorge often compared to a miniature Grand Canyon, with viewpoints and a rim walking trail that require no technical climbing experience. Jebel Akhdar, the Green Mountain, sits at a high enough elevation to support terraced rose and fruit gardens in an otherwise arid country, and its cooler temperatures make it a popular escape during the hotter months when Muscat and the desert legs become less comfortable. Both are reachable by 4x4 from the Nizwa area and work well as an extension rather than a replacement for the core Muscat-Nizwa-Wahiba Sands route.

Wadi Bani Khalid: The Detail Most Itineraries Skip

Between Nizwa and the Wahiba Sands sits Wadi Bani Khalid, a series of natural turquoise pools fed by mountain springs, cool enough for a genuinely refreshing swim after a hot desert drive. Most rushed itineraries skip it entirely in favour of getting to the desert camp faster, but it costs little extra time to build in as a midday stop and consistently surprises first-time visitors who did not expect a natural swimming pool in the middle of an otherwise arid landscape.

Culture and Dress Code Notes

Oman is a conservative Muslim country, and while it is genuinely welcoming to tourists, a few practical notes go a long way toward a smoother trip.

  • Dress modestly in public, shoulders and knees covered for both men and women, particularly outside beach and resort areas.
  • Full coverage is required at the Sultan Qaboos Grand Mosque, including a headscarf for women, loose full-length clothing, and closed shoes; scarves are sometimes available to borrow on site but carrying your own avoids any delay.
  • Alcohol is available in licensed hotels and restaurants but not sold in general retail the way it is in Dubai, worth knowing if that is part of your trip planning.
  • Public displays of affection are best kept minimal, consistent with broader Gulf cultural norms.
  • Fridays follow a different rhythm, since it is the primary day of rest, with some souqs and businesses operating on adjusted hours, the Nizwa livestock souq specifically is a Friday-morning institution worth timing your visit around rather than against.

Packing and Practical Notes

  • Omani Rial is the local currency; cards are widely accepted in Muscat but carry cash for the Nizwa souq and desert camp.
  • Pack for a genuine temperature swing, hot desert days and noticeably cooler desert nights, particularly if visiting the Wahiba Sands or the Jebel Akhdar mountain extension.
  • A dedicated 4x4 with an experienced driver is essential for the Wahiba Sands and Jebel Shams legs, the terrain is not suited to a standard rental car, and most well-run itineraries include this as part of the package rather than leaving it to arrange locally.
  • English is widely spoken in Muscat's tourist areas, including hotels, restaurants, and major attractions, making on-the-ground communication straightforward for most travellers.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is there really a direct flight from Amritsar to Muscat?

Yes, Air India Express operates this route directly, avoiding the need to connect through Delhi or any other hub, a detail most generic Oman package content for this region does not mention.

Do Indians need a visa for Oman?

Yes, a standard e-visa applied for entirely online, with processing typically taking 24 to 48 hours. Oman has largely discontinued visa-on-arrival for Indian passport holders, so this needs to be arranged before travel.

Is Oman safe for Indian tourists, including families and solo travellers?

Yes, Oman is widely regarded as one of the safest countries in the Gulf region, with a stable, low-crime environment that suits families, solo travellers, and first-time Gulf visitors alike.

How many days does an Oman trip need?

Five to six days comfortably covers Muscat, Nizwa, and an overnight in the Wahiba Sands, the core experience most first-time visitors are after. Add extra days for a Jebel Shams mountain extension or a Salalah add-on if your schedule allows.

What is the best single experience in Oman for a first visit?

The overnight Wahiba Sands desert camp consistently rates as the highlight for first-time visitors, combining a 4x4 dune-bashing experience, a Bedouin-style camp, and genuinely dark night skies within a single, well-organised overnight stop.

Is Oman good for a honeymoon?

Yes, particularly the desert camp night and a Salalah extension if travel dates allow, both of which offer a quieter, more intimate experience than a typical city-and-beach Gulf itinerary.

Can I rent a car and drive myself in Oman?

Self-driving is possible on Muscat's main roads and the highway to Nizwa, but the Wahiba Sands and Jebel Shams legs require genuine off-road capability and local route knowledge, which is why most well-planned itineraries use a dedicated 4x4 with a driver for those specific stretches rather than a standard rental.

Is alcohol available in Oman?

Yes, but only within licensed hotels, resorts, and certain restaurants, not through general retail. This is a meaningfully more relaxed policy than some neighbouring countries but still more restricted than Dubai, worth factoring in if it matters to your trip plans.
