12 Weeks Out: Lock Your Dates and Start Watching Fares

At twelve weeks before Diwali, most people have not started thinking about travel yet, which is exactly why this is the best time to start. Decide roughly where you want to go and how many days you can take off. You do not need a firm booking yet, but you do need a firm date range, because every week you wait from here narrows your options and raises your likely fare. This is also the point to start a fare watch on your preferred route with our desk, the same approach we cover in detail in our cheap flights guide. Twelve weeks feels early to most people, and that instinct is exactly why so many festival trips end up costing more than they needed to. The travellers who move first are not being anxious. They are simply the ones who understand how festival demand actually behaves.

8-10 Weeks Out: Book Flights Now, Not Later

This is the single most important window on this entire calendar. Fares booked 60-90 days ahead of Diwali are consistently the cheapest available, and every week you wait after this point trades away savings. If your dates are fixed by family plans or leave approval, this is when you commit. Two tactical details worth knowing:

  • Flying on Diwali day itself (8 November) is often 20-40% cheaper than flying the Friday or Saturday before it, since most travellers want to be home for the actual festival evening rather than in transit.
  • Return flights on 12 or 13 November tend to be the best-value return dates, just after the festival crowd has already gone home.

If you are travelling as a family group or planning a joint trip with relatives, this is also the point to ask about group fare blocks, which we cover in our group travel desk.

6-8 Weeks Out: Confirm Hotels and Visas

With flights locked, hotel rooms in popular Diwali destinations begin filling fast, particularly anywhere with its own festival draw. If your trip is international, this is also the last comfortable window to file a visa application without rushing processing. For destinations we have covered in detail, cross-check the current entry rules before booking non-refundable stays, especially given how quickly rules can shift, as our Thailand visa update showed earlier this year.

4 Weeks Out: The Last Comfortable Booking Window

By four weeks out, the easiest fares and room categories are already gone, but a good trip is still very achievable. This is the point where we start actively working around constraints rather than simply booking the obvious choice. If your first-choice flight has gone up sharply, we look at nearby dates, alternate routings through Delhi, or a different departure time that still gets you home for the festival.

2 Weeks Out: Damage Control, Not Panic

If you are reading this with two weeks left, the message is simple: it is late, not hopeless. Fares will be higher and the best hotels will be gone, but options still exist, particularly if you have some flexibility on exact dates or are open to a less obvious destination. This is exactly where a ticketing desk that tracks live inventory daily earns its keep over a portal search that only shows what is left rather than what could still work with a small adjustment.

Why Festival Fares Behave Differently From Normal Fares

On a normal route, fares mostly follow the booking-window pattern we cover in our cheap flights guide: book four to six weeks out and you generally do fine. Diwali breaks that pattern because demand is not spread across the month, it is compressed into a handful of specific days. Airlines know exactly which dates the whole country wants to fly on, and fare buckets on those dates empty out far faster than on an ordinary week. This is why the 60-90 day window matters more for Diwali than for almost any other trip you will book all year. It is not a general rule of thumb here, it is the difference between a normal fare and a genuinely inflated one.

What Actually Happens to a Route You Wait On

It helps to see this as a real pattern rather than an abstract warning. A route that shows a comfortable, ordinary fare in early August will typically show a noticeably higher fare by mid-September, purely because the cheapest seat buckets have sold. By late October, the same route often has only the highest fare classes left, and by the first week of November, some flights on the peak days are simply full. None of this happens as one dramatic jump. It happens in small increments every week, which is exactly why it is easy to underestimate until the week you actually try to book.

How This Is Different From a Normal Long Weekend

Our weekend getaways guide covers the general 2026 long-weekend calendar, and much of that logic still applies here, but Diwali deserves its own treatment for one reason: it is the single highest-demand travel week of the entire Indian calendar, well above Republic Day, Independence Day or even most wedding-season weekends. That scale changes the maths. A booking strategy that comfortably works for an ordinary long weekend, such as booking three to four weeks out, simply does not hold for Diwali, where the same timeline often means choosing from whatever fares happen to be left rather than the fare you actually wanted.

Diwali Booking Mistakes We See Every Year

  • Waiting for a sale that never quite arrives. Diwali is peak season everywhere in India. Airlines rarely need to discount a route that will sell out anyway.
  • Booking the outbound flight without checking the return date's price. A cheap outbound on a bad return date can cost more overall than a slightly pricier outbound paired with a smart return.
  • Ignoring tier-2 routing options. Sometimes flying via a slightly less obvious connection, rather than the first result a search shows, saves meaningfully during peak weeks.
  • Assuming hotel rooms will still be there. Popular Diwali destinations, especially Amritsar and hill towns, genuinely sell out their best properties weeks ahead.
  • Forgetting visa timelines for international trips. A visa that normally takes a few days can slow down during any high-demand travel season, so file early rather than assuming standard processing speed will hold.

Packing and Practical Diwali Travel Notes

  • Carry sweets and gifts in checked luggage, not hand baggage, since many airlines restrict certain packaged food items and loose sweets in the cabin, and checked bags avoid the question entirely.
  • Firecrackers cannot be carried on any flight, checked or hand baggage, under standard aviation safety rules. Buy them locally at your destination if that is part of your celebration plan.
  • Book airport transfers in advance for late Diwali-night arrivals, since taxi availability in many cities drops sharply on the festival evening itself as drivers head home to celebrate.
  • Keep a printed copy of hotel bookings and ID for every traveller, particularly for group trips, since front desks during peak season move faster with paperwork ready rather than searched for on the spot.
  • If travelling with elderly family members, confirm wheelchair or priority boarding assistance at the time of booking rather than at the airport, since festival-week staff are stretched thinner than usual.

What This Costs to Get Wrong

The honest version of this guide is simple: nothing about Diwali travel is complicated, but the cost of delay compounds quietly. A route that is comfortably affordable at ten weeks out can be significantly pricier at four weeks out, not because of any single dramatic price jump, but because each week a few more of the cheapest seats and rooms disappear. Nobody notices the individual week that mattered. Everyone notices the total by the time they are booking in a hurry. The fix is not complicated. It is simply acting on this countdown at whichever stage you are reading it, rather than filing it away for later and rediscovering it in October with fewer options left.

Frequently Asked Questions

When is Diwali in 2026?

Diwali falls on Sunday, 8 November 2026. The full five-day festival window runs from Dhanteras on 6 November to Bhai Dooj on 10 November.

Is it actually cheaper to fly on Diwali day itself?

Often, yes. Since most travellers prioritise being home before the festival evening, flights on 8 November itself can run 20-40% cheaper than the Friday or Saturday immediately before it, when demand peaks hardest.

How far in advance should I book Diwali flights?

What if I am only booking a few weeks before Diwali?

It is late, not hopeless. Fares and room categories will be more limited, but a live desk that checks actual current inventory can usually still find a workable option, particularly with a little flexibility on exact dates.

Should I book Diwali flights and hotels separately or together?

Together, ideally, and through one desk. Booking a flight without confirming hotel availability for the same dates risks landing a great fare with nowhere good left to stay, particularly in smaller Diwali-popular towns where room inventory is limited. We check both before confirming either.

Do visa rules change or slow down during Diwali season?

Processing speed does not officially change, but volume does, since many travellers file applications in the same weeks. Filing your visa application as early as possible in the countdown, rather than assuming standard turnaround will hold under higher demand, is the safer approach for any international Diwali trip.
