This guide answers both before getting into the logistics.

Quick context: Rafting in Rishikesh runs from roughly mid-September to June 30, with the monsoon months closed for safety. Routes range from short day trips of a few kilometres to multi-day expeditions covering more than 80 kilometres, and the right choice depends far more on your group's experience than on how adventurous the trip sounds.

The honest rule: if you have never rafted before, start with a shorter stretch. Multi-day expeditions are a great experience, but attempting one as a first-time rafter, without the confidence built on a shorter day, is a common reason people find the trip harder than they expected.

Choosing a Licensed Operator

There are hundreds of operators in Rishikesh, and quality varies considerably. Check for a valid licence and clear safety standards before you book, not after. Ask specifically whether life jackets, helmets, and a trained guide are included for every rafter, not just the lead boat, whether the operator provides safety briefings before departure, and whether the river permits and forest entry fees are included in the quoted price or listed as extras.

The honest read: the cheapest option on a booking site is not always the best value. A well-run operator with proper equipment, a clear briefing, and experienced guides is worth a modest premium, particularly on the medium and longer stretches where the river's conditions demand more.

Getting There from Chandigarh

Rishikesh is roughly five to six hours from Chandigarh by road, through Haridwar, making it one of the easiest weekend adventure trips from the Tricity. There are no direct flights, the nearest airport is Dehradun's Jolly Grant Airport, roughly an hour's drive from Rishikesh, but most Chandigarh-based travellers drive directly, which also gives flexibility on departure time and luggage. Haridwar is a natural stop on the way up, with the Ganga Aarti at Har ki Pauri worth building into a first evening.

A Sample Two-Day Weekend

  • Day 1: Drive from Chandigarh to Rishikesh via Haridwar, evening Ganga Aarti at Har ki Pauri if your timing allows, check in at a riverside hotel or campsite
  • Day 2: Morning safety briefing and rafting on your chosen stretch, lunch on the riverbank, drive back to Chandigarh in the afternoon

A three-day version works well for a medium or multi-day stretch, adding a rest day or a second activity like a short cliff-jumping session, a riverside cafe afternoon, or a walk along the Lakshman Jhula suspension bridge.

Safety Notes Every Rafter Should Know

  • Listen closely to the safety briefing, it covers paddling commands, what to do if you fall out, and how to position yourself in the raft, and skipping attention to it is one of the most common mistakes first-time rafters make.
  • Wear the life jacket and helmet at all times, even when the water looks calm, since conditions change quickly on the river.
  • Do not raft after heavy rain or on days the operator cancels, the river's condition after rainfall can be significantly more dangerous, and a responsible operator will postpone rather than push ahead.
  • Tell your operator about any health conditions beforehand, including heart or back issues, so the guide can recommend a suitable stretch.

What to Pack

  • Quick-dry clothing and a spare set in a dry bag, you will get wet, and cotton stays cold and heavy once soaked.
  • Sturdy sandals or water shoes with a heel strap, not flip-flops, which tend to come off in the rapids.
  • Sunscreen and a cap, the sun reflects strongly off the water.
  • A waterproof phone pouch, though most operators advise leaving valuables at the base camp rather than carrying them on the raft.

Beyond the River: What Else Rishikesh Offers

Rishikesh is more than a rafting base, and a weekend built around the river often benefits from a few non-water activities for rest days or for group members who are not paddling.

  • Lakshman Jhula and Ram Jhula, the two famous suspension bridges across the Ganga, walkable and photogenic, especially in the late afternoon
  • Parmarth Niketan and the riverside ashrams, a calmer side of Rishikesh, with evening aarti ceremonies open to visitors
  • Beatles Ashram, a deserted meditation complex that has become a genuinely popular visit, with its own history and street art
  • Cafe culture along the riverbank, a relaxed way to spend an afternoon between rafting sessions or after a multi-day expedition

Rafting Gear and What Operators Usually Provide

Most licensed operators include helmets, life jackets, paddles, and a trained guide in each boat within the rafting package itself, and it is worth confirming this is the case rather than assuming. Some operators offer optional add-ons such as wetsuits or specialised footwear, worth requesting if the weather is cool or the stretch is longer. Your own clothing and personal items remain your responsibility, which is why the packing list above matters more than most first-time rafters expect.

Planning a Group Trip Without the Usual Headaches

Group rafting trips from the Tricity tend to go smoothly when a few things are settled before the day itself. Confirm the exact headcount with the operator early, since raft sizes and guide allocation depend on it. Collect ID and any health information from every participant before departure, not on the morning of the activity. Agree on who is responsible for splitting costs and handling payments before you travel, since group arrangements are much simpler when money questions are settled in advance. Build in a relaxed evening rather than a packed agenda, since rafting is physically tiring and group energy tends to drop sharply after a long day on the water.

Frequently Asked Questions

When is the rafting season in Rishikesh?

Roughly mid-September to June 30. The monsoon months, July and August in particular, are closed for safety.

Is rafting safe for beginners?

Yes, on shorter stretches with a licensed operator, a proper safety briefing, and a trained guide in every boat. Start with a shorter route and build up to longer stretches once you are comfortable.

How long is the drive from Chandigarh?

Roughly five to six hours by road, through Haridwar, making it a manageable weekend trip from the Tricity.

What is the best stretch for a first-time rafter?

A short stretch in the roughly 9 to 16 kilometre range, which gives a good introduction to the river without the sustained rapids of longer routes.

Do I need to know how to swim?

It helps, and operators typically recommend it, though life jackets are mandatory and a guide is in every boat. Tell your operator if you are not confident in the water so they can advise on the most suitable stretch.

Is rafting in Rishikesh suitable for children?

Short stretches are generally suitable for children old enough to follow the safety briefing and sit securely in the raft, with the minimum age and height requirements set by each operator. Confirm these with your operator before booking, since they vary.

What happens if the river is too high on the day of my booking?

A responsible operator will postpone or reschedule rather than run the route in unsafe conditions. Build a buffer day into your trip if you can, since weather-related changes are a normal part of river rafting.

Can I combine rafting with other activities in the same trip?

Yes, Haridwar's Ganga Aarti, the Lakshman Jhula bridge, and short riverside activities like cliff jumping or a cafe afternoon all pair well with a rafting day.
