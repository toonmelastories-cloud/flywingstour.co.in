Nepal is visa-free for Indians, and that fact leads almost every article about travelling there, which is exactly why the actual document requirement catches people off guard. At Flywings Tour & Packages Pvt Ltd, we have had more than one client assume their Aadhaar card would work for air travel to Kathmandu, the same way it works for a domestic flight, only to find out at check-in that it does not. This guide starts with that correction, since it is the single most avoidable way a simple, visa-free trip goes wrong.

Quick context: For air travel to Nepal, Indian citizens need an original physical passport or Election Commission Voter ID, nothing else works. Aadhaar, PAN, and driving licences are not accepted, and photocopies or scanned images of any document are not accepted either.

The Document Rule, Stated Plainly

Travel ModeWhat You Actually Need
Air travel (Chandigarh or Delhi to Kathmandu)An original, physical Indian passport or Election Commission Voter ID card. No visa required, but one of these two documents is mandatory.
Overland border crossingThe original Voter ID is accepted in place of a passport at land checkpoints, following the same physical-document-only rule.
Not accepted, at any crossingAadhaar card, PAN card, driving licence, and any photocopy, scan, or digital image of a passport or Voter ID

This rule exists specifically because Nepal's visa-free arrangement with India relies on verifying citizenship through one of these two documents in original form, not because of any general suspicion of other Indian ID types. Knowing this before you book flights, not at the airport counter, is the entire point of this section.

Getting There from Chandigarh

There is no direct flight from Chandigarh to Kathmandu. Delhi is the practical hub, with Air India running a substantial non-stop schedule, roughly 42 weekly flights, and a flight time of about an hour and a half. From Chandigarh, that means a short domestic hop to Delhi followed by one direct international leg, genuinely one of the shorter international connections available from this region.

The Circuit

Kathmandu, 2 to 3 Days

The cultural core of most Nepal itineraries. The Boudhanath and Swayambhunath stupas, both major Buddhist pilgrimage sites, the Pashupatinath Temple, one of the most significant Shiva temples outside India, and Kathmandu Durbar Square's palace and temple complex together anchor a proper two to three day stay. Pashupatinath specifically draws a large number of Indian pilgrims given its religious significance, worth building extra time around if that is part of your trip's purpose.

Pokhara, 2 to 3 Days

A dramatically different pace from Kathmandu, set beside Phewa Lake with the Annapurna range as a backdrop. Boating on the lake, paragliding over the valley, and the Sarangkot viewpoint for sunrise over the mountains make Pokhara the adventure and relaxation counterpart to Kathmandu's cultural focus. This is also where more serious trekkers base themselves before heading into the Annapurna region, though a standard leisure itinerary does not need to go beyond the town itself.

Chitwan, 1 to 2 Days

Nepal's oldest national park, and a genuine wildlife draw, home to one of the last populations of single-horned rhinoceros alongside Bengal tigers and a wide range of bird species. A jungle safari here adds a completely different texture to the trip compared to Kathmandu's temples and Pokhara's lake, well suited to travellers who have already covered similar Himalayan cultural circuits and want something distinct.

A Sample 7-Day Itinerary

  • Day 1: Arrive Kathmandu, settle in, evening at Thamel
  • Day 2: Boudhanath, Pashupatinath, Kathmandu Durbar Square
  • Day 3: Swayambhunath, Patan Durbar Square, drive or fly to Pokhara
  • Day 4: Phewa Lake boating, Sarangkot sunrise the following morning
  • Day 5: Pokhara at leisure, optional paragliding, drive to Chitwan
  • Day 6: Full Chitwan day, jungle safari, cultural programme in the evening
  • Day 7: Return to Kathmandu for departure

This routing covers all three destinations without excessive backtracking, and works whether you drive between cities or use Nepal's reasonably efficient domestic flight network to save time on the Kathmandu-Pokhara leg specifically.

What to Eat

Nepali food shares real common ground with North Indian cuisine while carrying its own distinct dishes, a comfortable food culture for most Indian travellers to adjust to immediately.

  • Momos, Nepal's signature dish, steamed or fried dumplings filled with meat or vegetables, found everywhere from street stalls to proper restaurants and genuinely considered a Nepali specialty rather than a borrowed Tibetan snack at this point
  • Dal bhat, the everyday meal structure, lentil soup, rice, and vegetable curries, similar in spirit to a North Indian thali but with its own distinct spice profile
  • Newari cuisine, Kathmandu Valley's indigenous food tradition, worth seeking out specifically in the old city areas for dishes rarely found outside the valley
  • Sel roti, a ring-shaped sweet rice bread, traditionally made for festivals but available year-round from local vendors

Vegetarian travellers generally do well in both Kathmandu and Pokhara's tourist areas, given the overlap with familiar North Indian vegetarian cooking traditions.

Packing and Practical Notes

  • Nepali Rupee is the local currency, though Indian Rupees are widely accepted in tourist areas, though notes above a certain denomination sometimes face restrictions, worth carrying a mix of smaller denominations.
  • Carry your original passport or Voter ID at all times during the trip, not just at the airport, since it may be requested at hotels and certain checkpoints.
  • Layer for genuine temperature variation between Kathmandu, Pokhara, and Chitwan, the valley cities run cooler than Chitwan's lower-altitude, warmer climate.
  • Domestic flights between Kathmandu and Pokhara are worth booking rather than the full road journey if your trip is on the shorter end, saving meaningful time versus the roughly six to seven hour drive.

Nepal for Family and Group Trips

The Kathmandu-Pokhara-Chitwan circuit suits family groups well, Kathmandu's temples and Pokhara's lake are both manageable for a wide age range, and Chitwan's jungle safari format works for children old enough to sit through a multi-hour excursion. For pilgrimage-focused groups specifically, Pashupatinath draws large numbers of Indian visitors, and coordinating timing around less crowded hours, early morning specifically, matters more for a comfortable group experience than for an individual visit.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do Indians need a visa for Nepal?

No, entry is visa-free for Indian citizens for tourism, business, and pilgrimage, governed by the long-standing India-Nepal Treaty of Peace and Friendship.

Can I travel to Nepal using my Aadhaar card?

No. Aadhaar, PAN, and driving licences are not accepted for travel to Nepal. You need an original, physical Indian passport or Election Commission Voter ID card, and photocopies or digital scans of either are not accepted.

Is there a direct flight from Chandigarh to Kathmandu?

Not from Chandigarh directly, but Delhi has extensive direct connectivity to Kathmandu, roughly 42 weekly Air India flights alone, at about an hour and a half flight time, so the trip is a short domestic hop to Delhi followed by one direct international leg.

How many days does a Nepal trip need?

Seven days covers Kathmandu, Pokhara, and Chitwan comfortably. Four to five days works for a Kathmandu-and-Pokhara-only trip that skips the Chitwan wildlife extension.

Is Nepal good for a pilgrimage trip specifically?

Yes, particularly around Pashupatinath Temple in Kathmandu, one of Hinduism's most significant Shiva temples, drawing a substantial number of Indian pilgrims each year alongside its role as a broader tourist destination.

What is the biggest planning mistake people make with Nepal specifically?

Assuming Aadhaar will work as identification the way it does for domestic Indian travel. It does not, and confirming you have an original passport or Voter ID ready well before your travel date avoids the single most common and entirely avoidable issue with an otherwise simple trip.

Can children travel to Nepal without their own passport?

Children also need their own original passport or Voter ID, the same identity document rule applies regardless of age, worth confirming well ahead of travel if a child in your group does not yet have either document.

Is Nepal safe for solo and women travellers?

Yes, Nepal is generally considered a safe and welcoming destination for tourism, including for solo and women travellers, with a well-established tourist infrastructure across the main Kathmandu-Pokhara-Chitwan circuit.

Can this trip be combined with trekking in the Annapurna region?

Yes, Pokhara is the standard base for Annapurna region treks, and travellers with additional days and genuine trekking experience can extend a standard leisure itinerary into a proper multi-day trek, though this needs separate planning and gear beyond a standard sightseeing trip.
