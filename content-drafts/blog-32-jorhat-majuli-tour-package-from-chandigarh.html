Two Places, One Genuinely Different Trip

Jorhat and Majuli sit close together geographically, a short ferry ride apart, but they deliver almost entirely different experiences, which is why we are treating them as a paired trip rather than one destination with two names.

  • Jorhat is Assam's tea capital, colonial-era planter bungalows, working tea estates open to visitors, and a genuine window into the industry behind the cup of Assam tea most Indian households drink daily.
  • Majuli is the world's largest river island, formed by the Brahmaputra, and the spiritual heart of Assamese Vaishnavism, home to living monasteries called Satras that have preserved centuries-old dance, music, and mask-making traditions.

Most first-time visitors expect one or the other to be the "main" destination and the second an add-on. In practice, they earn roughly equal time, and skipping either one leaves a real gap in the trip.

Getting There from Chandigarh

There is no direct flight from Chandigarh, but the routing is more straightforward than it looks. Delhi to Guwahati runs on multiple daily non-stop flights across several airlines, and Guwahati to Chandigarh is itself well served with around 20 daily flights on the return leg, meaning Guwahati functions as a genuine two-way hub rather than a one-directional stopover. From Guwahati, Jorhat is reachable either by a short domestic flight into Jorhat's own Rowriah Airport, connected by non-stop flights from Guwahati and Kolkata, or by a scenic road journey of around five to six hours if you would rather see the Brahmaputra valley on the way in.

The Ferry: The Detail That Makes or Breaks This Trip

This is the single most important practical detail in this entire guide, more than flights, more than hotels. Majuli is reached from Jorhat via a ferry from Nimati Ghat, and getting this timing wrong is the most common way a well-planned Jorhat-Majuli trip goes sideways.

DetailWhat to Know
Crossing timeRoughly 1.5 hours from Nimati Ghat to Majuli
Typical operating windowFerries generally run between 8 AM and 4 PM, with the first departure from Majuli around 7:30 AM and the last around 3:15 PM
FareModest, typically in the range of 15 to 30 rupees per person for a standard passenger ferry
Schedule reliabilityGenuinely inconsistent. Most ferries do not run on a fixed published timetable the way a city bus does, and timings shift with river conditions
Monsoon riskJuly to September sees frequent flooding-related disruption, with services occasionally suspended entirely for days at a time

The practical takeaway: never plan a same-day return crossing as your only option, and never schedule your Majuli visit for the July-to-September window if you can avoid it. Build in a buffer day, confirm the day's ferry schedule locally the morning of travel rather than trusting an online timetable, and treat any Majuli itinerary as needing at least one overnight stay on the island rather than a rushed day trip.

What to Do in Jorhat

  • Tea estate visits, several working estates around Jorhat welcome visitors for guided walks through the plantation, a tour of the processing factory, and a proper tasting session, a genuinely different experience from simply drinking tea at home
  • Colonial planter bungalow stays, a number of heritage tea-estate bungalows now operate as boutique accommodation, offering a level of old-world charm that is hard to find outside this specific region
  • Kaziranga National Park, roughly a two-hour drive away, home to the one-horned rhinoceros and a natural extension for anyone with an extra day or two, though it deserves its own dedicated write-up rather than a rushed add-on

What to Do in Majuli

  • Satra visits, the island's monasteries are living institutions, not museum pieces, and visitors can observe monks practising traditional dance and music forms that trace back to the 15th-century Vaishnavite reformer Srimanta Sankardev
  • Mask-making workshops, Majuli's mukha shilpa tradition received a Geographical Indication tag in March 2024, a formal recognition of a craft found essentially nowhere else in India, and several Satras welcome visitors to watch the process
  • Cycling the island, Majuli is flat, rural, and genuinely rewarding to explore by bicycle, rice paddies, small villages, and river views without the need for a vehicle
  • Sunset at the riverbank, simple, unstructured, and consistently rated by visitors as one of the most memorable parts of the trip

A Sample 6-Day Itinerary

  • Day 1: Arrive Guwahati, connect to Jorhat by flight or road, evening at leisure
  • Day 2: Full Jorhat day, tea estate tour and tasting, planter bungalow stay
  • Day 3: Morning ferry to Majuli, check in, first Satra visit
  • Day 4: Full Majuli day, mask-making workshop, cycling the island, sunset at the riverbank
  • Day 5: Morning ferry back to Jorhat, optional half-day Kaziranga detour if time allows, or a relaxed final Jorhat morning
  • Day 6: Return to Guwahati for departure

This pacing gives both halves of the trip proper time and builds in the ferry-schedule flexibility this route genuinely needs rather than assuming a tight same-day crossing will work as planned.

What to Eat

Assamese food is distinct from both mainland Indian and the rest of Northeast Indian cuisine, built around simplicity, fresh river fish, and a lighter use of oil and spice than most Indian regional cooking.

  • Assamese thali, typically rice, dal, a fish curry, and a mix of seasonal vegetable preparations, often finished with khar, a distinctive alkaline preparation unique to the region
  • Fish curry (masor tenga), a tangy, light fish preparation, often made with tomato or elephant apple, a genuine regional speciality worth seeking out over a generic hotel menu
  • Pitha, rice-based sweet and savoury cakes, particularly associated with Assamese festivals but available year-round in local eateries
  • Assam tea itself, obviously, and a proper tasting session at a working estate in Jorhat is a genuinely different experience from a teabag at home
  • Betel nut and paan, offered as a customary gesture of hospitality in many Assamese homes and at Satras, worth trying at least once if offered

Vegetarian travellers are reasonably well catered for in Jorhat's hotels and restaurants, though Majuli's food options are simpler and more limited given the island's smaller scale, worth flagging dietary needs to your homestay or guide in advance.

Practical and Packing Notes

  • Carry cash, particularly for Majuli, where card acceptance is limited outside the larger guesthouses, and small ferry and local transport fares are cash-only.
  • Book Majuli accommodation in advance during the October-to-March peak, since the island's homestay and guesthouse capacity is genuinely limited compared to a mainstream hill-station destination.
  • Dress modestly when visiting Satras, and be mindful that some monastery areas restrict photography during active prayer or practice sessions, worth checking with your guide before assuming it is allowed.
  • Mobile network coverage on Majuli is patchy, genuinely part of the island's appeal for anyone wanting to properly disconnect, but worth planning around if you need to stay reachable.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a permit needed to visit Jorhat or Majuli?

No, Indian tourists need no Inner Line Permit for either destination, just a valid photo ID, unlike Tawang or parts of Nagaland where permits are required.

Is there a direct flight from Chandigarh to Jorhat?

Not from Chandigarh directly. The trip routes through Guwahati, which has strong flight connectivity in both directions, followed by a short domestic flight or a five-to-six-hour road journey to Jorhat.

How many days does a Jorhat-Majuli trip need?

Six days covers both destinations comfortably, including at least one overnight stay on Majuli itself rather than a rushed day trip. Four days works for a shorter version that skips the Kaziranga extension.

Is the Majuli ferry safe and reliable?

Generally safe, but not reliable in the way a scheduled city service is. Timings shift with river conditions and are not always published accurately in advance, which is why we recommend confirming the day's schedule locally rather than trusting an online timetable, and avoiding the July-to-September monsoon window entirely.

What is Majuli's mask-making tradition?

Known as mukha shilpa, it is a centuries-old craft practised in the island's Satras, used to create elaborate masks for traditional dance and drama performances tracing back to the 15th-century Vaishnavite reformer Srimanta Sankardev. The craft received a Geographical Indication tag in March 2024.

Can this trip be combined with Kaziranga National Park?

Yes, and it is a natural extension for wildlife enthusiasts, roughly a two-hour drive from Jorhat. Add at least one extra day if including it, since the park deserves proper time rather than a rushed half-day stop.

Is Jorhat-Majuli safe for solo and women travellers?

Yes, Assam is generally considered a safe state for tourism, and the Jorhat-Majuli circuit specifically sees a warm, welcoming reception given how genuinely pleased locals are that visitors are discovering the region. As with any offbeat destination, sticking to established homestays and guides rather than fully improvised arrangements is the sensible approach.

What should first-time visitors know that generic guides miss?

That the ferry schedule genuinely is not fixed the way most travel content implies. Build slack into the itinerary around the crossing rather than treating the published timetable as reliable, and this becomes a smooth, memorable trip rather than a stressful one.
