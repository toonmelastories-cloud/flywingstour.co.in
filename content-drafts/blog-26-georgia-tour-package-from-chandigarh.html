What Changed for Indian Travellers in 2026

If the last Georgia guide you read was written before this year, it is missing two things that now matter.

What's NewWhat It Means for You
DuVerify identity re-verificationAfter submitting your standard e-visa application, you receive a DuVerify link by email and must complete an additional identity verification step, along with a separate fee, within 24 hours. Skipping this step stalls your visa application entirely.
Mandatory travel insuranceFrom January 1, 2026, every tourist entering Georgia must carry health and accident insurance with a minimum coverage of roughly 30,000 GEL (approximately USD 11,000). This is checked, not just recommended.

Beyond these two additions, the core e-visa process is still straightforward for Indian passport holders: an entirely online application, no embassy visit required, a passport valid at least three months beyond your Georgia visa with at least two blank pages, and typical processing of five to seven working days. Budget for both the visa fee and the DuVerify fee when you apply, and start the process a little earlier than you might have last year to account for the extra step.

The Application, Step by Step

  1. Submit the standard e-visa application online, with your passport scan, a recent photo, and basic travel details, roughly USD 20 for the visa fee itself.
  2. Watch for the DuVerify email, which arrives after submission and contains a link to complete the additional identity verification step, along with its separate fee of roughly USD 15.
  3. Complete DuVerify within 24 hours of receiving the link. Missing this window is the single most common reason a straightforward application ends up delayed.
  4. Arrange your travel insurance meeting the minimum 30,000 GEL coverage requirement before you travel, and keep proof of it accessible, not just booked.
  5. Receive your approved e-visa, typically within five to seven working days of a clean application, and carry a printed copy alongside the digital one.

The Three-Leg Circuit

Almost every well-planned Georgia trip is built around the same three legs, and five days is the generally recommended minimum to do all three without feeling rushed.

Leg One: Tbilisi, 2 to 3 Days

Start and end here, since it is also where your flight lands. Old Tbilisi's balconied streets, the Narikala Fortress cable car, the Dry Bridge flea market, and the city's sulfur bathhouses are the anchor sights, but the real draw for most first-time visitors is simply the cafe culture and the food, which is unlike anything else on a typical Indian traveller's international shortlist.

Leg Two: Kazbegi and Gudauri, 1 to 2 Days

A drive north along the Georgian Military Highway, stopping at Ananuri Fortress on the way, leads to Gudauri, a mountain town that has become a favourite for Bollywood and Punjabi film shoots, and on to Stepantsminda, better known as Kazbegi. The Gergeti Trinity Church, perched at over 2,100 metres with Mount Kazbek behind it, is the single most photographed spot in the country and the reason most people add this leg in the first place.

Leg Three: Kakheti Wine Region, 1 Day

Back toward Tbilisi and then east into Kakheti, Georgia's wine country. A family winery tour with a qvevri cellar tasting, lunch of traditional Kakhetian food, and an afternoon in the walled hill town of Sighnaghi rounds out the circuit. This leg surprises a lot of first-time visitors, since Georgia's wine-producing history predates most of Europe's, and the tastings are unpretentious in a way European wine country visits often are not.

Why Punjabi Travellers Specifically Are Drawn to This

This is not marketing spin, it comes up unprompted in almost every conversation we have with a client back from Georgia. Georgian hospitality culture, insisting a guest leave the table full, toasting extensively at every meal, treating a stranger at a family winery like an old friend, maps onto Punjabi hospitality customs closely enough that visitors consistently describe the feeling as familiar rather than foreign. Add mountain scenery that genuinely rivals Himachal and Kashmir in a completely different architectural setting, and it is easy to see why Georgia has moved from a niche pick to a genuine competitor against repeat Dubai or first Europe trips for this specific audience.

What to Eat

Georgian food deserves its own mention because it consistently surprises first-time visitors, and it is a genuine part of the trip rather than an afterthought.

  • Khinkali, large soup dumplings meant to be picked up by the twisted knot at the top and eaten by hand, traditionally not with cutlery
  • Khachapuri, a boat-shaped cheese bread, the Adjaruli version topped with a raw egg and butter stirred in tableside is the one to try first
  • Mtsvadi, skewered grilled meat, usually pork or beef, served at almost every family meal and winery lunch
  • Churchkhela, a walnut-and-grape-must candy sold everywhere from street stalls to airport shops, an easy souvenir to bring home
  • Qvevri wine, fermented in large clay vessels buried underground, a method UNESCO-recognised as a distinct Georgian winemaking tradition and very different in taste from anything most Indian travellers have tried

Vegetarian travellers generally do well here, Georgian cuisine has a strong tradition of vegetable, bean, and cheese dishes independent of any meat course, though it is worth flagging dietary preferences to your guide or hotel in advance since English menus are not universal outside Tbilisi.

Packing and Practical Notes

  • Layer for mountain weather even in summer, since Gudauri and Kazbegi sit at altitude and can be noticeably colder than Tbilisi on the same day.
  • Carry your insurance documentation and DuVerify confirmation printed or easily accessible, not just saved in an email you might not have signal to open on arrival.
  • Georgian Lari is the local currency; cards are widely accepted in Tbilisi but carry some cash for smaller wineries and mountain-town stops.
  • English is spoken reasonably well in Tbilisi's tourist areas but less so in Kakheti and the mountain towns, where a guide genuinely adds value beyond just logistics.

A Note on Batumi, the Fourth Leg Most Itineraries Skip

We mention Batumi as an optional extension rather than a core leg deliberately, since most first-time Georgia itineraries genuinely do not need it and adding it without extra days just compresses the three legs that matter more. That said, if your trip runs seven days or longer, Batumi's Black Sea promenade, its slightly surreal modern architecture next to Ottoman-era old town streets, and its botanical garden are worth the extra day, particularly for travellers who want at least one coastal day built into an otherwise mountain-and-city itinerary. It connects to Tbilisi by a roughly six-hour train ride or a short domestic flight, both reasonably comfortable options.

Who Georgia Genuinely Is Not For

We would rather say this plainly than oversell a destination that is not right for everyone. If your idea of a good trip is beach time above everything else, Georgia is landlocked apart from the Batumi extension, and Batumi's Black Sea coast is a pleasant add-on rather than a Maldives or Bali replacement. If nightlife and resort infrastructure matter more to you than scenery and culture, Dubai or Bangkok will suit better. And if mobility is a concern, the Kazbegi leg in particular involves a fair amount of walking and uneven mountain terrain around the church itself, worth flagging honestly rather than discovering on the day. For most other travellers, particularly first-timers to the region and honeymooners specifically, it delivers exactly what the recent surge in interest suggests it does.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do Indians need a visa for Georgia?

Yes, most Indian travellers need a standard e-visa, applied for entirely online. Visa-free entry only applies to Indian passport holders who already hold a valid visa or residence permit from a specific list of countries including the US, UK, Schengen Area, or GCC states.

What is the DuVerify requirement?

An additional identity verification step introduced in 2026 for e-visa applicants, completed via a link sent by email after your initial application, along with a separate fee, and required within 24 hours to keep your visa application moving.

Is travel insurance really mandatory for Georgia now?

Yes, since January 1, 2026, every tourist entering Georgia must carry health and accident insurance with a minimum coverage of approximately 30,000 GEL. This is checked at entry, not optional.

Is there a direct flight from Chandigarh to Georgia?

Not from Chandigarh directly, but IndiGo now flies non-stop from Delhi to Tbilisi, so the trip is a short Chandigarh-Delhi domestic hop followed by one direct international leg, with no second connection required.

How many days does a Georgia trip need?

Five days is the generally recommended minimum to properly cover Tbilisi, the Kazbegi mountain leg, and the Kakheti wine region without rushing. Add two or three more days if you want to extend to Batumi on the Black Sea coast.

Is Georgia good for a honeymoon?

Yes, and it is one of the fastest-growing honeymoon picks among our Chandigarh clients specifically, combining mountain scenery, old-town charm, and wine country in one trip at a noticeably lower cost than an equivalent Western European itinerary.

Is Georgia safe for Indian tourists, including solo and women travellers?

Yes, Georgia is generally considered one of the safer destinations in the region for tourists, including solo and women travellers, with low reported crime rates in the main tourist circuit. As with any international trip, standard precautions around late-night mountain drives and sticking to established tour operators for the Kazbegi 4x4 leg apply.

Can vegetarian Indian travellers eat comfortably in Georgia?

Generally yes. Georgian cuisine has a strong independent tradition of vegetable, bean, and cheese dishes such as lobio and various khachapuri styles, though flagging dietary needs to your guide in advance helps, particularly outside Tbilisi where English menus are less common.
